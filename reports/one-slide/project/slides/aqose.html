AQoSE

Odds, not fixed thresholds: a predictive add-on for Linux CAKE

Fixed thresholds mistake calls and games for downloads. AQoSE predicts what each connection is from its first 8 packets, weighs the odds against the measured harm of each queue, and picks the safest. CAKE still moves every packet; AQoSE only labels flows and sets rates. Never reads content.

Adaptive QoS Engine

WHAT IT
DOES

1

Listens

to the first 8 packet headers of every new connection. No payload, no decryption.

2

Predicts

what it is: call, game, video call, streaming, web, download or backup, with odds.

3

Decides

the CAKE queue with the least expected harm, inside hard safety guards.

4

Watches

every second; re-checks flows that change, rolls back anything that adds delay.

Runs locally, no cloud. Falls back to plain CAKE in 2 s if it stops. Tested in emulation.

How it was tested: 9 emulated home situations × 6 router setups (no QoS, plain CAKE, cake-autorate, threshold rules, top label, AQoSE), 10 seeds of identical traffic each; then, after the system was frozen, 5 fresh seeds, a new scenario and replayed real WhatsApp calls. Paired statistics, bootstrap intervals, A/A noise band. Limits: one laptop, no real router or Wi-Fi yet; generated calls don't lower their bitrate when squeezed; weaker on apps from sources never seen in training.
