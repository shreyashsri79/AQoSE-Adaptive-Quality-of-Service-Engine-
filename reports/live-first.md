# First live comparison, night of Oct 12

**A first look, not a result.** One seed per scenario. The classifier is `lgbm-draft3-d70c179b`, trained on a draft dataset. The damage table behind O2 had one seed per cell and two of its three conditions. B4 runs qosify's own thresholds. S5 and S10 ran here for the first time.

What it shows, in one paragraph: where the interactive flow shares its device with bulk traffic (S6, S7) plain CAKE and the threshold rules leave it broken and the classifier fixes it. Where the link's capacity drops (S2) the capacity estimator fixes what a fixed shaper cannot. Where every interactive flow is alone on its device (S1, S3, S4) all four tie. In S5 top label beat expected damage, because the damage table was missing the case; in S10 the fallback for silent probes hurt a call on a link that was fine. Both are written up in the project log, section 3.

| Scenario | B2, plain CAKE | B4, threshold rules | O1, classifier by top label | O2, classifier by expected damage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 download during a call | fine | fine | fine | fine |
| S2 link drops to a fifth | call broken | call broken | fine | fine |
| S3 three TVs and a console | fine | fine | fine | fine |
| S4 backup during a game | fine | fine | fine | fine |
| S5 work call on a busy laptop, with a lease | call broken | call and game broken | fine | call broken |
| S6 HD call on a laptop sharing files | call broken | call broken | fine | fine |
| S7 cloud game on a PC fetching an update | game broken | game degraded | fine | fine |
| S10 probes silenced, then controller killed | fine | fine | call degraded during the first fault | call degraded during the first fault |

In S10 the watchdog put the router back to plain CAKE 2.02 to 2.03 s after the controller was killed, in all three controller configurations.
